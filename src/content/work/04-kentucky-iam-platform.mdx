Most engineers who build something for one organization expect it to stay there. That is usually right. A school district's provisioning is shaped by its HR system, its student system, its naming habits, and a decade of local decisions, and none of that travels well.

I think ours traveled because we solved a problem every district already had, and solved it in a way that didn't depend on being Warren County.

## Scripts to System

Before the statewide rollout, many Kentucky districts provisioned accounts by hand. Many others ran on some mix of temperamental scripts and small, siloed solutions.

The Kentucky Department of Education was already looking for something better. After we launched the delta sync at Warren County in July 2023, KDE and Identity Automation, the company behind Rapid Identity, asked to use our system as the basis for the state's.

## System to State

"Use our system" meant all of it. Each district got its own Rapid Identity tenant, and each tenant ran the same code and configuration:

- the **action sets** that do the provisioning work
- the **source mappings** from Munis (HR) and Infinite Campus (student data)
- the **group logic** that decides who gets access to what
- the **naming rules** for usernames and email addresses
- the **delta sync design**: poll for changes all day, then run a full sync each evening

A tenant per district kept every district's data separate, while still letting one design serve all of them.

## State to Standard

One piece changed. At Warren County, I pulled student data from Infinite Campus with a direct database connection. That wasn't a principled choice; it simply made the architecture simpler. The state wanted to standardize on the OneRoster API instead, which makes sense when you're connecting 171 districts rather than one.

OneRoster alone didn't cover everything we needed, though. So the statewide version pairs it with an Infinite Campus write-back action set, which sends each student's username and email back to Infinite Campus and provisions staff data that originates in Munis.

## Standard to Support

My part of the rollout was advisory. I met with KDE and Identity Automation weekly or every other week, and many more times with Identity Automation's architecture team. I also handed over documentation along with the code.

A good share of those meetings went into how district setups differed. The conclusion we reached was that districts didn't need their own version. They could either improve their setup by adopting ours, or keep operating the way they were within our design.
