Most access requests at a school district aren't hard. A contractor needs an account. A technician needs admin rights in Entra ID for a project. A new hire lost the link to claim their account. Each one is a small ticket, and each one used to mean someone making the change by hand, and someone else remembering to undo it.

## The idea

We already had logic that synced group memberships across our connected apps. So instead of granting access app by app, I made group membership the thing that grants it. Put someone in the group, and the apps that trust that group give them access. Take them out, and it goes away.

## The request form does the rest

The piece that made it work was Rapid Identity's request service. A request form adds or removes a person from a group automatically, and that change flows through the same sync that provisions everything else. I wrote the logic behind those requests.

That covered more than I expected:

- **Contractor enrollment**, without a technician building each account
- **Elevated access**, like Entra ID admin roles, granted through a request and revoked the same way
- **Account-claim links**, which HR can now resend on their own instead of opening a ticket for a new hire

## Why groups

Revoking access is the part people forget. When access is tied to a group, removing it is one change, not a checklist across every app someone touched. The same mechanism that grants access takes it away, so the two can't drift apart.
